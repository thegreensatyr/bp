How to Schedule Bluesky Posts for Multiple Brands

If you run more than one brand or persona on Bluesky, here's how to actually keep the queues, voices, and accounts separate — not just technically possible, but easy to do by habit.

BrandParent · Updated November 2026

Bluesky doesn't require developer approval to connect a scheduler — you generate a free app password from your own account settings, and any tool that supports it can post on your behalf. That makes it one of the easiest platforms to schedule for, and also one of the easiest to get wrong if you're running two or more brands through the same scheduling tool.

Step 1: Get an app password per account

In Bluesky, go to Settings → Privacy and Security → App Passwords and generate one. If you run multiple Bluesky accounts (one per brand), repeat this for each account you plan to connect — don't reuse a single app password across brands, since that makes it harder to revoke access to just one later.

Step 2: Connect each account to its own brand, not a shared pool

This is the step most schedulers get wrong: they treat every connected account as one shared pool you post into, with the brand distinction living only in your memory of which account is which. In BrandParent, each brand gets its own cubicle, and an account connected inside one cubicle is never visible to another — there's no shared account list to pick the wrong one from.

Step 3: Keep each brand's queue separate

Every cubicle keeps its own "Up next" queue. Scheduling a post for one brand never shows up in another brand's calendar, and there's no master schedule that merges them — which matters once you're running three or four brands and can't hold every upcoming post in your head at once.

Step 4: Check what you're posting before it goes out

Bluesky posts are capped at 300 graphemes, and images over 1MB get automatically recompressed. Video only works if your Bluesky email is verified. None of that is brand-specific — but the brand-specific risk is posting the right content from the wrong account, which is a habit problem, not a platform problem.

If you're only running one brand, any scheduler handles this fine. The complexity shows up specifically when you're context-switching between two or more voices — that's the actual problem worth solving for.
